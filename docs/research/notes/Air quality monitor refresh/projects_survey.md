# Survey of DIY/open-source and commercial indoor air quality monitors (as of late November 2026)

Context for the reader: the user's 2021 build is ESP8266 Wemos D1 mini + SCD40/41 (CO2) + SGP40 (VOC) + SPS30 (PM) + BMP280 + 1 NeoPixel + Nextion touch display, ESPHome/Home Assistant, through-hole breakouts on a 2-layer PCB. Everything below was gathered from web sources fetched on 2026-11-25. Where a source is an aggregator, retailer, or forum post rather than a primary source, that is said explicitly.

## Open-source / DIY projects: sensors, display, MCU, enclosure, PCB approach, firmware, price, open-source status

### Takeaway
Two patterns dominate DIY in 2025–2026. The first is AirGradient's discrete-sensor approach: SenseAir S8 NDIR + Plantower PMS5003 + SGP41 + SHT40 on an ESP32-C3 PCB, CC-BY-SA 4.0, OSHWA-certified. The second, and newer, is a wave of community builds around Sensirion's all-in-one **SEN66** (PM1–PM10, CO2, VOC/NOx index, T/RH in one 55×26×21 mm module) on an ESP32-C3/C6/S3. These builds use ESPHome and small e-ink, round-LCD, or large touch-LCD screens in 3D-printed cases. Apollo AIR-1 (SEN55 + SCD40, ESPHome) and M5Stack Air Quality v1.1 (SEN55 + SCD40 + 1.54" e-ink, official ESPHome package) are the main "ready-made but hackable" options. No confirmed hardware successor to the AirGradient ONE or Apollo AIR-1 was found as of late 2026.

### Inferences
- The user's 2021 sensor set (SCD4x + SGP40 + SPS30 + BMP280) maps almost one-to-one onto a **SEN66 + pressure sensor** design, which is where the 2025–2026 community has moved. The trade-off: the SEN66's integrated T/RH is specified at ±0.45 °C / ±4.5 %RH, much looser than a discrete SHT4x (±0.2 °C). It also draws ~90 mA continuously, a waste-heat source in itself. AirGradient's choice of a discrete SHT40 and an NDIR S8 is the "accuracy-first" alternative.
- HCHO (SEN68/SEN69C or SFA30/SFA40) and pressure-trend displays (Project Aura) are the most common "more sensor types" additions. An accelerometer and RTC (Air Lab) appear on portable devices.
- For a factory-assembled PCB, the closest open references are AirGradient (CC-BY-SA, full schematics and 3D files) and FeatureNAB (KiCad + JLC PCBA files). Project Aura shows the opposite "no PCB" approach, at the cost of cable clutter and dependence on breakout stock (Adafruit SEN6x breakout was out of stock).

### Gaps
- No primary-source confirmation of an AirGradient ONE successor or SEN66 variant; the official page shows only I-9PSL.
- Licenses for Apollo AIR-1, FeatureNAB, Project Aura, and doctormord repos were not captured.
- Air Lab's actual open-source release (hardware/firmware) as of late 2026 was not verified (Crowd Supply page returned 403).
- LilyGO, Waveshare, and DFRobot packaged air-quality kits: none found in searches. Tindie and Crowd Supply beyond Air Lab were not systematically surveyed.
- AirGradient's PCB assembly specifics (which sensors are SMD on-board vs plug-in modules) were not confirmed beyond the S8 and PMS5003 being modules.

## Commercial reference points: sensors and industrial design

### Takeaway
The best-regarded consumer devices cluster into three types. The first is battery e-ink "glanceable" devices (Aranet4 with Senseair Sunrise NDIR; Airthings View Plus with a 2.9" e-ink). The second is mains-powered touch-LCD hubs (Qingping Air Monitor 2 with Sensirion SCD40/SGP40/SHT4x). The third is minimalist LED-ring or LED-bar devices (PurpleAir Touch, AirGradient). The 2026 entrants found are cheap Matter devices (Sonoff AirGuard CO2, $49.90, 3.73" LCD) and HVAC-tied monitors (AprilAire ZAWTHCPV). Independent AQ-SPEC data shows large PM accuracy spread among consumer devices: AirGradient Open Air 0.97–0.98 R², uHoo 0.

## Recurring design patterns in the best-reviewed devices

### Takeaway
Small footprint (roughly 60–100 mm per side) with USB-C power is the norm. A single dominant readout is backed by an LED or ring for at-a-glance status. Displays go e-ink for battery or bedroom use, OLED or round LCD for small mains builds, and 4"+ touch LCD for "hub" devices. Sensors and the PM fan path are physically separated from the MCU and display electronics, with vents top and bottom.

## Lessons and criticisms reported by reviewers and builders

### Takeaway
The most consistent lesson is **self-heating**: MCU (especially ESP32), display backlight, and sensor heaters bias temperature (and therefore RH) upward. Sensirion recommends a design with under 5 °C over-temperature before relying on software compensation. Next come index semantics (the VOC/NOx index is relative to roughly the past 24 h, not absolute), cloud dependence in commercial devices, e-ink ghosting and refresh trade-offs, and PM-sensor fan wear and noise.

### Cited Findings
- **Sensirion SEN6x temperature compensation app note (v1.1, Jan 2026)**: the STAR-Engine compensates waste-heat offset, warm-up time constant (τ63), ambient-dependent slope, and thermal-mass lag ("acceleration"). Variation between units scales with the size of the self-heating, so mechanical integration should be optimized first, and "an over-temperature of less than 5 °C is recommended" before compensating. Any change in operating mode (e.g., screen brightness, lights) changes waste heat and invalidates the calibration. Characterize the device on a wooden plate simulating a desk. Reference sensors go ≥20 cm from heat sources, not above the device. Acceleration preset for an IAQ monitor: "Light (IAQM)" T1=100, T2=300, K=20, P=20 (air purifier: 250/550/150/20). — [Sensirion app note PDF](https://sensirion.com/media/documents/C964FCC8/69709EC3/PS_AN_SEN6x_Temperature_Compensation_and_Acceleration_Application_No.pdf)
- **SCD4x offset**: ESPHome's default `temperature_offset` is 4 °C. The offset depends on measurement mode, self-heating of nearby components, ambient temperature, and airflow — [ESPHome SCD4x](https://esphome.io/components/sensor/scd4x/). Community: temperature "very accurate… as long as I don't put the sensor in my enclosure. Heat from the ESP32 is causing misreadings". An ESP32-C6 build read ~1 °C high, fixed with a −0.8 °C offset — [HA forum (via search summary)](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/esp32-temperature-reading-errors/975098). A guide recommends a separate SHT4x for room T/RH and keeping the SCD41 for CO2 only — [esp32.co.uk](https://esp32.co.uk/esp32-indoor-air-quality-station-scd41-sgp40-pms5003-sht45/) (blog)
- **Sensor-to-sensor heating**: on the SenseCAP Indicator, SCD41 T/RH readings are "compromised" because the SCD41 sits next to the heated SGP40 — [ESPHome devices](https://devices.esphome.io/devices/seeed-sensecap/)
- **Apollo AIR-1**: the ESP32-C3 generates heat, so T/RH offsets may be needed. An estimated-room-temperature model is available but off by default. A "sawtooth" CO2 pattern appears when the device is oriented "Apollo side up"; flipping it fixes this. The DPS310 was discontinued mid-production. — [Apollo FAQ](https://wiki.apolloautomation.com/products/air1/faq/), [GitHub](https://github.com/ApolloAutomation/AIR-1)
- **VOC/NOx index semantics**: Sensirion's VOC index is relative to the environment's recent history (baseline estimated over ~24 h) and "does not represent absolute concentrations". 100 = typical for that room, range 1–500. ### Inferences
- For a redesign that keeps "accuracy first": place the T/RH reference sensor (SHT4x) in its own vented pocket upstream of the airflow, away from the ESP32, regulator, display backlight, and any MOx heater (SGP4x). Keep the device's total over-temperature under about 5 °C, per Sensirion. Calibrate offsets with the display at its normal brightness. A Wemos-class ESP8266 or ESP32 next to an SCD4x inside a closed 3D-printed box is the most-reported failure mode.
- On-screen VOC/NOx index values should be labeled as relative (e.g., "VOC index 100 = typical"), not shown as ppb or µg/m³.
- If using a SEN66, its 90 mA draw plus the ESP32 makes active thermal management or STAR-Engine compensation (with its offset/slope/τ63 parameters) more important than with discrete low-power sensors.
- E-ink suits a slow-changing glanceable IAQ display if partial-refresh ghosting is handled (periodic full refresh). LCD or touch suits richer UIs but adds backlight heat and needs a night/brightness mode.

### Gaps
- No quantitative fan-noise comparison (dB) between SPS30, PMS5003, and SEN6x was found.
- No independent long-term drift study of SCD4x vs SenseAir S8 was retrieved. AirGradient's own sensor-comparison blog posts were not located in this session.
- The Sensirion SEN6x Mechanical Design and Assembly Guidelines (vent and placement geometry) were referenced but not retrieved.
- Burn-in reports for the OLED (AirGradient) or LCDs in IAQ monitors were not found.
